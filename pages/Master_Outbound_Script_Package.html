0. Campaign Context (Locked from Discovery)

  • Offer, in plain language: help nonprofits build the earned-income engine — enrollments, appointments, memberships — that funds their mission on their own terms.
  • Priority sequence: enrollment marketing first, mental health client-pipeline building second.
  • Buyer: Executive Director, President, or other director-level decision-maker.
  • ICP: nonprofit budget $1M–$50M; small universities, mental-health therapy nonprofits, trade organizations; US now, Canada next.
  • Disqualifiers: no marketing budget, no program capacity to absorb new demand, or outside the ICP above.
  • Standing guardrail: no guarantees, ever. Stay highly professional at all times. Do not blend in the separate B2B offer.
  • Success definition: a qualified appointment is a quick no or a real conversation toward a decision — not a stall.

If the target shifts (industry, buyer, offer), only Section 0 and the bracketed variables need to change — the psychology and structure carry over.

1. Cold Call Script

Objective

Earn 20 seconds of permission, surface whether the org is structurally dependent on restricted funding, and move a genuinely relevant prospect toward a booked meeting — without pitching services.

Psychology

Executive directors are pattern-trained to hang up on agency pitches within the first five words. The opening has to signal self-awareness ("you weren't expecting this call") and ask for permission rather than assume it. Permission-based openers measurably lower the guard because they hand control back to the prospect.

Script

Rep: "Hi [Name] — this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. I'll be honest, this is a cold call and you weren't expecting it. Do you have twenty seconds?"
[If yes] Rep: "Appreciate it. We work with [nonprofit type — e.g., mental health nonprofits / small universities] on growing the revenue they generate directly — enrollments, appointments, memberships — so they're less boxed in by grant and donor restrictions. I'm reaching out to a handful of similar organizations to see if that's on your radar. Is that something you and your team have been wrestling with lately?"
[If engaged] Rep: "Can I ask — is it more that [enrollment / appointment volume] has softened, or that the funding you do have comes with more strings than it used to?"
[Listen. Reflect back in one sentence. Then:] Rep: "That's exactly the kind of thing we help with. Rather than get into it on a cold call, would it be worth fifteen minutes with [our team] to see if there's a fit? No pressure either way — if it's not relevant, you'll know in the first five minutes."

Why It Works

The permission ask filters out hostile responses early. The one diagnostic question does the qualifying instead of a features pitch. "No pressure either way" mirrors the client's own no-guarantees, non-pushy standard and lowers resistance to booking.

Alternative Opener (Referral / Warm Angle)

Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact — we just wrapped a project with [comparable org, e.g., a mental-health nonprofit] and I thought of [Org Name] because you're a similar size and mission. Got twenty seconds?"

2. Gatekeeper Version

Objective

Get past the front desk or assistant without sounding like a vendor cold call, and without asking the gatekeeper to make a judgment call they're not equipped to make.

Psychology

Gatekeepers are trained to screen out sales calls, not colleagues. Speaking briefly, using a first name only, and asking a direct routing question reads as internal or peer traffic rather than outbound sales.

Script

Rep: "Hi, this is [Rep] — quick one for you. Who handles growth or enrollment strategy over there, [Name] or someone else?"
[If routed to voicemail or told to email] Rep: "That's great, thank you — could I get [Name]'s direct line, or should I go through you for a callback?"
[If pressed on reason for calling] Rep: "Sure — we help nonprofits like yours build revenue outside of grants and donations. I just want thirty seconds with whoever owns that."

Why It Works

Asking a routing question rather than pitching gives the gatekeeper nothing to screen. Naming a real function ("growth or enrollment strategy") signals legitimacy without naming a sales motive.

3. Voicemail

Objective

Leave a message that earns an open on the follow-up email — not one that tries to close on its own.

Psychology

Long voicemails get deleted. A short, specific, no-ask message that promises a follow-up email reduces the cognitive load on the prospect and gives them a lower-effort way to respond (reply to email vs. call back a stranger).

Script

Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. We work with organizations like [Org Name] on growing enrollment and appointment revenue outside of grants and donations. I'll send a short email over so you have something concrete to look at — if it's relevant, I'd welcome fifteen minutes. Talk soon."

Why It Works

No cliffhanger, no false urgency — consistent with the client's professional, no-guarantees tone. The voicemail's only job is to make the email feel expected rather than cold.

4. Objection Handling

Responses below are reframes, not rebuttals — the goal is to keep the prospect talking, not to win the exchange.

ObjectionResponseWhy It Works
"Not in this year's budget." "Completely fair — most of the organizations we work with weren't budgeting for it either. What usually changes their mind is that the program pays for itself once it's running, so it's less a new cost and more a new revenue line. Worth fifteen minutes to see if the math would work for you?" Reframes budget as a documented objection the client already flagged; positions the offer as self-funding rather than a new expense.
"We already have a marketing agency / handle it internally." "Good to hear you're already investing in it. Most teams we talk to are focused on branding or donor marketing — we're narrowly focused on earned revenue: enrollments, appointments, memberships. Different lane, so it's worth a quick look even alongside what you already have." Avoids positioning as a replacement, which lowers loyalty-based resistance, and narrows the offer to a gap competitors rarely cover.
"We're too busy / don't have the bandwidth." "That's actually the exact reason clients bring us in — the goal isn't to hand your team another project, it's to be a multiplier so you're not adding headcount. Think of it as a team of ten for less than the cost of one hire." Directly reuses the client's own documented reframe, so messaging stays consistent across every SDR.
"Send me some information." "Happy to — I'll send a short one-pager over now. While I've got you, can I ask one question so what I send is actually relevant: is the bigger gap enrollments, appointments, or member growth?" Complies with the request (builds trust) while extracting one more qualifying data point instead of letting the call end with nothing.
"We're not looking right now." "Understood, and I won't push. Would it be reasonable to check back in a quarter, or is there a better time of year for you, budget-cycle-wise?" Protects the relationship for future re-engagement instead of forcing a close, consistent with the no-pressure standing rule.

5. Discovery Questions

Use two to three of these per call — never all of them. Pick based on where the prospect showed energy.

  1. What does a strong month look like for you right now — enrollments, appointments, or new members?
  2. How much of your current budget is grants and donations versus revenue you generate directly?
  3. If that ratio doesn't shift, what does the next twelve months look like for your team?
  4. What's slowed you down in the past when you've tried to grow that side of the organization?
  5. Have you worked with an outside team on this before? What worked, and what didn't?
  6. Who else would need to be in the room if this moved forward?

6. Qualification Checklist

An appointment only counts as qualified if every box below is checked. Ties directly to the ICP in Section 0 and the Discovery Summary.

  • US-based nonprofit (Canada only if explicitly pre-approved)
  • Annual budget between $1M and $50M
  • Org type: small university, mental-health therapy nonprofit, or trade/membership association
  • Has an active marketing budget or clear willingness to allocate one
  • Has program capacity to absorb new enrollments / appointments / members (i.e., not disqualified for lack of infrastructure)
  • Contact is Executive Director, President, or another director-level decision-maker
  • Pain point confirmed in the prospect's own words (enrollment, appointments, or membership shortfall)
  • No conflicting exclusivity with a competing agency for this specific offer

7. Meeting Booking Transition

Objective

Convert a warm, qualified conversation into a booked meeting without sounding like a pivot into "sales mode."

Script

Rep: "Based on what you're describing, I think this is worth a real conversation rather than me guessing on a cold call. I'd like to get you on a call with [Name/Title] — they can walk through what this has looked like for organizations like [Comparable Org] and you can decide from there if it's worth pursuing. I've got [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] — which works better?"

Why It Works

Framing the meeting as "so you can decide" rather than "so we can pitch you" keeps the peer-to-peer tone and avoids triggering last-minute resistance right before the ask.

8. Follow-Up Email

Objective

Give the prospect something concrete to reference after the call or voicemail, written the way one operator writes to another.

Template

Subject: Following up — [Org Name] and unrestricted revenue

Hi [Name],

Following up on my voicemail / our call. We work with organizations like yours on growing the revenue you generate directly — enrollments, appointments, memberships — so less of your budget is tied to grant or donor restrictions.

A quick example: [Comparable proof point, e.g., a mental-health therapy nonprofit increased appointment revenue significantly within a year of starting].

If it's useful, I'd like to grab fifteen minutes to see whether something similar could apply at [Org Name] — no pressure if the timing isn't right.

[Calendar link]
[Rep name / title / phone]

Why It Works

Short, specific, one proof point, one soft ask. No urgency language or guarantees, consistent with the client's standing tone rule.

9. Follow-Up LinkedIn Message

"Hi [Name] — I left you a voicemail / we spoke briefly about growing enrollment and appointment revenue at [Org Name]. Wanted to connect here in case it's easier to keep track of. Happy to share what's worked for similar organizations whenever it's useful."

Why It Works

Low-commitment ask (a connection, not a meeting) keeps the relationship open for prospects who aren't ready to book yet.

10. Follow-Up SMS

Use only after a prior conversation or explicit opt-in — never as a cold-outreach channel.

"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] from Earned Impact — following up on our call. Sent an email with a quick example of what we've done for similar organizations. Let me know if it's worth 15 min."

Ideas for the Hook — "Earned Revenue Growth Blueprint"

Purpose: increase meeting acceptance by replacing generic discovery calls with a high-value, customized deliverable that prospects receive simply for attending.

The Offer: every qualified prospect receives a custom "Earned Revenue Growth Blueprint" for their organization — a strategic overview, not a sales pitch.

Deliverable Outline

  1. Snapshot: current programs, audiences, revenue model, and initial observations.
  2. Growth Opportunities: specific paths (e.g., enrollment, partnerships, membership).
  3. Revenue Constraints: bottlenecks (e.g., dependency on grants, weak pipelines).
  4. Quick Wins: three immediate, actionable recommendations.
  5. Sample Strategy: a high-level concept (audience, channel, and messaging).

Why This Works

  • For Prospects: they receive concrete value regardless of whether they become a client.
  • For Earned Impact: it differentiates us from generic agencies, increases meeting conversion, and shifts conversations from "sales" to "consultative."

Positioning Statement

"Join us for a 20-minute strategy session, and we'll prepare a customized Earned Revenue Growth Blueprint for your organization. You'll walk away with actionable recommendations — whether we work together or not."
