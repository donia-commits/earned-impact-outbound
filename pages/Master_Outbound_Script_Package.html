Campaign Context (Locked From Discovery)

OfferHelp nonprofits build the earned-income engine — enrollments, appointments, memberships — that funds their mission on their own terms.
PriorityEnrollment marketing first; mental health client-pipeline building second.
BuyerExecutive Director, President, or other director-level decision-maker.

Every script below is written to this context. If the target shifts (industry, buyer, offer), only Section 0 and the bracketed variables need to change — the psychology and structure carry over.

SECTION 1

Cold Call Script

Objective

Start a real conversation, understand the organization's current reality, and diagnose fit — with the meeting, not the cold call, as the place the solution actually gets discussed.

Psychology

The first minute should feel like discovery, not positioning. Naming Earned Impact's solution too early triggers the same pattern-recognition that gets agency calls hung up on. Leading with an observation about a shared theme across nonprofits — and letting the prospect talk more than the rep — builds the sense that this is a conversation between peers, not a pitch.

  1. 1
    Open — permission, not pitch
    Rep: "Hi [Name] — this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call and you weren't expecting it. Do you have twenty seconds?"
  2. 2
    Bridge to a shared theme — delay the pitch
    [If yes] Rep: "Appreciate it. We've been talking with quite a few nonprofits recently, and one theme keeps coming up — a lot of teams are working with constrained resources and still leaning hard on grants and donor dollars to grow. Does that sound familiar for [Org Name]?"
  3. 3
    Ask, don't tell
    Rep: "How are you currently growing unrestricted revenue — enrollment, appointments, membership, whatever that looks like for you — versus what's still tied to grants or donations?"
  4. 4
    Summarize, then ask again
    Rep: [Reflect back what was said in one sentence — e.g.,] "So it sounds like enrollment's held up, but the donor side has gotten less predictable." "Has that become a bigger conversation internally?"
  5. 5
    Let it expand, summarize once more
    [Prospect expands] Rep: "That makes sense — a lot of what we're hearing is teams wanting to build more of an earned-income engine so they're not as dependent on restricted funding, without having to add headcount to get there."
  6. 6
    Optional proof — one sentence, only if it fits

    See Section 7 for vertical-matched proof blocks. Insert at most one, right here, only if it fits naturally.

  7. 7
    Invite to a working session, not a pitch
    Rep: "Would it be worth putting fifteen minutes on the calendar — not a pitch, just a working session to see whether there's something here worth pursuing?"
Why it works

Each step earns the right to the next one. The prospect is doing most of the talking by Step 4, so the meeting ask in Step 7 lands as a natural next step in a conversation they've been driving — not a close the rep has been steering toward.

Alternative opener (referral / warm angle)
Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact — we just wrapped a project with [comparable org] and I thought of [Org Name] because you're a similar size and mission. Got twenty seconds?"
SECTION 2

Gatekeeper Version

Objective

Get past the front desk or assistant without sounding like a vendor cold call, and without asking the gatekeeper to make a judgment call they're not equipped to make.

Psychology

Gatekeepers are trained to screen out sales calls, not colleagues. Speaking briefly, using a first name only, and asking a direct routing question reads as internal or peer traffic rather than outbound sales.

Script
Rep: "Hi, this is [Rep] — quick one for you. Who handles growth or enrollment strategy over there, [Name] or someone else?"
[If routed to voicemail or told to email] Rep: "That's great, thank you — could I get [Name]'s direct line, or should I go through you for a callback?"
[If pressed on reason for calling] Rep: "Sure — we help nonprofits like yours build unrestricted revenue outside of grants and donations. I just want thirty seconds with whoever owns that."
Why it works

Asking a routing question rather than pitching gives the gatekeeper nothing to screen. Naming a real function ("growth or enrollment strategy") signals legitimacy without naming a sales motive.

SECTION 3

Voicemail

Objective

Leave a message that earns an open on the follow-up email — not one that tries to close on its own.

Psychology

Long voicemails get deleted. A short, specific, no-ask message that promises a follow-up email reduces the cognitive load on the prospect and gives them a lower-effort way to respond (reply to email vs. call back a stranger).

Script
Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. We work with organizations like [Org Name] on building sustainable, unrestricted revenue — enrollment, appointments, membership — without adding headcount. I'll send a short email over so you have something concrete to look at — if it's relevant, I'd welcome fifteen minutes. Talk soon."
Why it works

No cliffhanger, no false urgency — consistent with the client's professional, no-guarantees tone. The voicemail's only job is to make the email feel expected rather than cold.

SECTION 4

Objection Handling

Never argue and never "overcome" an objection — reframe it in one or two sentences and hand it back as a question, the way someone who's had this exact conversation hundreds of times would.

ObjectionResponseWhy It Works
"Not in this year's budget.""Yeah, that's what most people tell us at first — until they realize it tends to pay for itself once it's running, so it ends up being less of a new cost and more of a new revenue line. Is budget the only piece, or is there more to it?"Reframes budget as self-funding rather than a new expense, then hands the conversation back with a question instead of pressing for a yes.
"We already have a marketing agency / handle it internally.""Good — sounds like that's already getting some attention. Most of what we see internally or from agencies is more brand and donor communications; we sit in a narrower lane, just enrollment, appointment, and membership revenue. Is that piece actually being covered today, or more of a gap?"Doesn't position Earned Impact as a replacement, which avoids loyalty-based defensiveness, and turns the objection into a diagnostic question.
"We're too busy / don't have the bandwidth.""That tracks — most teams we talk to are already stretched thin. The way clients tend to think about it is less 'another project' and more a way to do more without adding headcount. Does bandwidth feel like the real blocker, or is it more that you haven't tried something like this before?"Reuses Damon's own "without increasing headcount" language, then separates the stated objection from what might actually be underneath it.
"Send me some information.""Happy to send something over. So it's actually useful instead of generic — what's the bigger gap right now, enrollment, appointments, or membership growth?"Complies with the request first, which builds trust, then uses it to extract one more qualifying detail rather than letting the call end with nothing.
"We're not looking right now.""Totally fair, no pressure at all. Would it make sense to check back in a few months, or is there a point in your budget cycle that's better timing?"Protects the relationship for future re-engagement instead of forcing a close, consistent with the no-guarantees, no-pressure standing rule.
SECTION 5

Discovery Questions

Choose two to four total across these categories, based on where the prospect showed energy — never work through the full list on one call.

Current State
  • What does a strong month look like for you right now — enrollments, appointments, or new members?
  • What's changed in the last year on that front?
Revenue Model
  • How much of your current growth is grants and donor-driven versus revenue you generate directly?
  • How has that mix shifted over the last few years?
Growth Challenges
  • What's been the biggest challenge in growing the unrestricted side?
  • What's gotten in the way in the past when you've tried?
Internal Resources
  • How big is the team working on this day to day?
  • Is this something you're staffed for, or is it more ad hoc right now?
Marketing
  • What's currently in place — internal team, an agency, a mix?
  • How's that been working for you?
Decision Process
  • Who else would need to be involved if this moved forward?
  • How do decisions like this typically get made on your end?
Timing
  • Is this a live priority, or more of a next-budget-cycle conversation?
  • Has this become a bigger conversation internally recently?
SECTION 6

Qualification Checklist

An appointment only counts as qualified if every box below is checked. Ties directly to the ICP in Section 0.

Soft Qualification Guidance

Use these as internal read-checks during the conversation, not as questions to ask verbatim.

  • Authority — are we talking with someone who can move this forward, or would this need to loop in someone else first?
  • Budget — is there room to invest now, or would this need to be built into next year's plan?
  • Need — did the pain point come from them, in their own words, or is the rep assuming it?
  • Timing — is this a live priority, or something on a future horizon?
  • Capacity — could they actually absorb more enrollments, appointments, or members if this worked?

SECTION 8

Meeting Booking Transition

Objective

Make the invitation feel like a continuation of the conversation the prospect has been driving — a working session they're accepting, not a demo they're being sold into.

Script
Rep: "Based on what you're describing, this sounds worth a real conversation rather than me guessing on a cold call. I'd like to put you together with [Name/Title] for a working session — they'll walk through what this has looked like for organizations like [Comparable Org], and you can decide from there whether it's worth pursuing. I've got [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] — which works better?"
Why it works

"Working session" and "you can decide" keep the frame consultative rather than transactional, and avoid the jolt of a sudden pivot into sales mode right when the prospect is most engaged.

SECTION 9

Follow-Up Email

Objective

Give the prospect something concrete to reference after the call or voicemail, written the way one operator writes to another.

Template
Subject: Following up — [Org Name] and unrestricted revenue

Hi [Name],

Following up on my voicemail / our call. We work with organizations like yours on building sustainable, unrestricted revenue — enrollment, appointments, membership — without adding headcount to get there.

A quick example: [proof point from Section 7, matched to their vertical].

If it's useful, I'd like to grab fifteen minutes to see whether something similar could apply at [Org Name] — no pressure if the timing isn't right.

[Calendar link]
[Rep name / title / phone]
Why it works

Short, specific, one proof point, one soft ask. No urgency language or guarantees, consistent with the client's standing tone rule.

SECTION 10

Follow-Up LinkedIn Message

Template
"Hi [Name] — I left you a voicemail / we spoke briefly about building more unrestricted revenue at [Org Name]. Wanted to connect here in case it's easier to keep track of. Happy to share what's worked for similar organizations whenever it's useful."
Why it works

Low-commitment ask (a connection, not a meeting) keeps the relationship open for prospects who aren't ready to book yet.

SECTION 11

Follow-Up SMS

Use only after a prior conversation or explicit opt-in — never as a cold-outreach channel.

Template
"Hi [Name], this is [Rep] from Earned Impact — following up on our call. Sent an email with a quick example of what we've done for similar organizations. Let me know if it's worth 15 min."
