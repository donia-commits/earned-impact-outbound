## 1. ICP Psychology

**What they wake up thinking about:** Whether this year's renewal numbers will hold, and whether the pipeline of new members is strong enough to offset an aging base. Membership associations live and die by dues revenue and engagement — a flat or shrinking roster isn't a marketing footnote, it's an existential threat to the organization's relevance and its budget. Many of these leaders are also watching engagement metrics (event attendance, participation, renewal touchpoints) decline even among members who technically stay on the rolls.

**What success looks like to them:** Membership growing instead of flat or shrinking. A renewal rate that holds or improves. A membership base that isn't just older and smaller every year. Engagement that shows members are actually getting value, not just paying dues out of habit. Non-dues revenue that gives the organization room to invest in programs without leaning entirely on the renewal cycle.

**Why they'd take a cold call at all:** Because "we help you grow and retain membership without adding headcount" addresses the metric their board watches most closely. This buyer thinks in membership terms first — growth, renewals, engagement — and in "unrestricted" or "non-dues revenue" terms second, as the underlying financial outcome once you connect the dots.

**The language they naturally use:** Members, renewals, membership growth, engagement, dues, non-dues revenue, chapters, association, retention. They will talk candidly about an aging membership base or declining event turnout — these are common, non-defensive topics in this sector.

**What would make them defensive:** Being told their association is "dying" or irrelevant, being pitched as "another vendor selling lists," or hearing a pitch that sounds like general marketing services rather than something specific to membership growth and retention.

## 2. Campaign Overview

**Objective:** Book a working session with a Trade/Membership decision-maker whose organization has a real, current gap in membership growth, renewal, or engagement — leading with membership language, with non-dues/unrestricted revenue as the underlying outcome, not the opener.

**Priority:** Third-priority vertical. Same small-TAM, quality-over-quantity discipline as the other two — measure twice, cut once.

**Sequence:** Cold call → Voicemail → LinkedIn (manual, no automation) → Email. Always in that order, never skipping a step.

**Standing guardrails:**
- No guarantees, ever.
- Never say "agency," "sales," "customers," "subscribers," or "company." Say organization, mission, community, programs, members.
- Lead with membership growth, renewals, and engagement; non-dues/unrestricted revenue is the underlying outcome, introduced once the pain is confirmed.
- Stay consultative. Never argue an objection; reframe it and hand it back as a question.

## 4. Call Psychology (Why This Script Is Built This Way)

**Objective of the call:** Earn twenty seconds of permission, surface whether membership growth, renewal, or engagement is a live, named problem, and move a genuinely relevant prospect toward a working session — without pitching a service.

**Why it's built this way:** Association leaders are used to being pitched association-management software, event platforms, and list-rental vendors — all of which promise growth through a tool, not through strategy. The way through is to speak in the association's own metrics (membership count, renewal rate, engagement) and let non-dues/unrestricted revenue surface as the mechanism once the prospect has confirmed the pain themselves.

---

## 5. Cold Call Script

### Step 1 — Open: permission, not pitch
> Rep: "Hi [Name] — this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call and you weren't expecting it. Do you have twenty seconds?"

### Step 2 — Bridge to a shared theme
> [If yes] Rep: "Appreciate it. We've been working with organizations like [Org Name] on membership growth and retention — a lot of associations are seeing renewal rates get harder to hold onto, or an aging base that isn't being replaced fast enough. Does that sound familiar for [Org Name]?"

### Step 3 — Ask, don't tell
> Rep: "How's membership tracking right now — is it more that new-member growth has slowed, or that engagement and renewals are the harder problem?"

### Step 4 — Summarize, then ask again
> Rep: [Reflect back in one sentence — e.g.,] "So it sounds like renewals are holding, but new-member growth hasn't kept pace." "Has that become a bigger conversation with your board?"

### Step 5 — Let it expand, summarize once more
> [Prospect expands] Rep: "That's consistent with what we're hearing — a lot of associations want to build more non-dues, unrestricted revenue so growth doesn't rest entirely on the renewal cycle."

### Step 6 — Optional proof (one sentence, only if it fits)
See Section 7. Insert at most one, right here, only if natural — and only the generic (unnamed) version unless the named case study has been cleared for external use.

### Step 7 — Invite to a working session, not a pitch
> Rep: "Would it be worth putting fifteen minutes on the calendar — not a pitch, just a working session to look at whether there's a real opportunity to close that gap?"

**Why it works:** The prospect names their own membership problem before the rep proposes anything. Non-dues/unrestricted revenue is introduced only after the pain is confirmed — never as the opening hook.

**Alternative opener (referral / warm angle):**
> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact — we've been working with an association similar in size and mission to [Org Name], and I thought of you given the overlap. Got twenty seconds?"

## 6. Gatekeeper Version

**Objective:** Get past the front desk or assistant without sounding like a vendor cold call, and without asking them to judge something they're not equipped to judge.

> Rep: "Hi, this is [Rep] — quick one for you. Who handles membership growth or marketing strategy over there, [Name] or someone else?"

> [If routed to voicemail or told to email] Rep: "That's great, thank you — could I get [Name]'s direct line, or should I go through you for a callback?"

> [If pressed on reason for calling] Rep: "Sure — we help membership associations grow and retain members without relying entirely on the renewal cycle. I just want thirty seconds with whoever owns that."

**Why it works:** A direct routing question gives the gatekeeper nothing to screen, and naming a real function ("membership growth or marketing strategy") signals legitimacy without naming a sales motive.

**Note per Damon:** he has confirmed he is willing to take a meeting with someone who reports directly to the actual decision-maker, not only the decision-maker themselves.

## 7. Voicemail

**Objective:** Leave a message that earns an open on the follow-up email, not one that tries to close on its own.

> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. We've been working with organizations like [Org Name] on membership growth and retention — building more non-dues, unrestricted revenue without adding headcount. I'll send a short email over so you have something concrete to look at — if it's relevant, I'd welcome fifteen minutes. Talk soon."

**Why it works:** No cliffhanger, no false urgency — consistent with the client's professional, no-guarantees tone.

## 8. Objection Handling

Never argue, never "overcome" — reframe in a sentence or two and hand it back as a question.

| Objection | Response | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not budgeted for this fiscal year." | "That's what most associations tell us at first — until they see it tends to pay for itself once it's running, so it becomes less of a new cost and more of a new revenue line tied to membership growth. Is budget the only piece, or is there more to it?" | Reframes cost as self-funding rather than a new expense; hands the conversation back with a question. |
| "We already have a marketing team / an agency." | "Good — sounds like that's getting some attention already. Most of what we see internally or from agencies is event and communications support; we sit in a narrower lane — specifically membership growth and retention. Is that piece actually covered today, or more of a gap?" | Doesn't position Earned Impact as a replacement, avoiding loyalty-based defensiveness; narrows to the specific gap. |
| "Our team is too busy / doesn't have the bandwidth." | "That tracks — most teams we talk to are already stretched thin. The way clients tend to think about it is less 'another project' and more a way to grow membership without adding headcount. Does bandwidth feel like the real blocker, or is it more that you haven't tried something like this before?" | Reuses the "without adding headcount" framing; separates the stated objection from what might be underneath it. |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to send something over. So it's actually useful and not generic — what's the bigger gap right now: new-member growth, renewals, or engagement?" | Complies first (builds trust), then extracts one more qualifying detail. |
| "We're not looking right now." | "Totally fair, no pressure. Would it make sense to check back closer to your next renewal cycle, or is there a better point in your budget calendar?" | Protects the relationship for future re-engagement instead of forcing a close. |

## 9. Discovery Questions

Choose two to four total, based on where the prospect showed energy. Never work through the full list on one call.

**Current State**
- What does a strong month look like for you right now — new members, renewals, or engagement?
- What's changed on that front over the last year or two?

**Revenue Model**
- How much of your growth is dues-based versus non-dues revenue you generate directly?
- How has that mix shifted over the last few years?

**Growth Challenges**
- What's been the biggest challenge in growing or retaining membership?
- What's gotten in the way in the past when you've tried to fix it?

**Internal Resources**
- How is membership marketing handled today — dedicated staff, or split across other roles?
- If membership grew faster than expected, could your team keep up with onboarding and engagement?

**Decision Process**
- Who else would need to be involved if this moved forward — your board, a membership committee?
- How do decisions like this typically get made on your end?

**Timing**
- Is this a live priority heading into your next renewal cycle, or more of a future-budget-cycle conversation?
- Has this become a bigger conversation internally recently?

## 10. Qualification Checklist

An appointment only counts as qualified if every box below is checked.

- [ ] US-based association (Canada only if explicitly pre-approved)
- [ ] Annual budget between $1M and $50M
- [ ] Org type: trade or professional membership association with a recurring dues model
- [ ] Has an active marketing budget or a clear willingness to allocate one
- [ ] Has capacity to onboard/serve new members if membership grew
- [ ] Contact is Executive Director, CEO, Director of Membership/Marketing, **or** someone who reports directly to one of those roles
- [ ] Pain point (membership growth, renewal, or engagement gap) confirmed in the prospect's own words
- [ ] No conflicting exclusivity with a competing agency for this specific offer

**Soft qualification guidance** (internal read-checks, not verbatim questions):
- **Authority** — talking with someone who can move this forward, or would this loop in the board first?
- **Budget** — room to invest now, or would this need to be built into next year's plan?
- **Need** — did the pain point come from them, in their own words?
- **Timing** — live priority tied to a renewal cycle, or a future horizon?
- **Capacity** — could the team actually onboard and serve more members if this worked?

## 11. Meeting Transition

**Objective:** Make the invitation feel like a continuation of the membership-growth conversation the prospect has been driving.

> Rep: "Based on what you're describing, this sounds worth a real conversation rather than me guessing on a cold call. I'd like to put you together with [Name/Title] for a working session — they'll walk through what closing a gap like this has looked like for an association your size, and you can decide from there whether it's worth pursuing. I've got [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] — which works better?"

**Why it works:** "Working session" and "you can decide" keep the frame consultative, and the comparison stays to "an association your size" unless a named case study has been cleared for use.
