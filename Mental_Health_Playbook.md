## 1. ICP Psychology

**What they wake up thinking about:** Whether the grant that's up for renewal this cycle actually gets renewed, and what happens to their clients and staff if it doesn't. Federal and foundation grant competition has intensified, and many mental-health nonprofits are seeing flat or declining donor support even as demand for services rises. This buyer is not thinking about "marketing" — they're thinking about whether the organization can keep its doors open and its clinicians paid at a wage that doesn't push them out the door.

**What success looks like to them:** Appointment slots that are actually full — because every open slot is capacity the organization is already paying for and not monetizing. Fewer restricted dollars, more revenue the organization controls itself, so a single lost grant doesn't threaten payroll. Lower staff turnover, because compensation isn't perpetually squeezed by underfunding. A shorter gap between community need (the waitlist) and what the organization can actually deliver.

**Why they'd take a cold call at all:** Because "sustainable, unrestricted revenue so you're not one grant decision away from a crisis" speaks directly to the thing keeping them up at night. This buyer, more than the other two ICPs, is receptive to hearing about earned income and sustainability directly — it's the language their board and funders are already using.

**The language they naturally use:** Appointments, clients, client access, behavioral health, community impact, mission, waitlist, clinical capacity, sustainability. They will talk about burnout, staffing shortages, and funding uncertainty candidly, because it's their daily reality.

**What would make them defensive:** Being told their fundraising or grant-writing is inadequate, being pitched as "just another agency," or hearing "funding is harder" stated flatly, as if it were a foregone conclusion rather than a shared challenge Earned Impact is positioned to help solve.

## 2. Campaign Overview

**Objective:** Book a working session with a Mental Health decision-maker whose organization has a real gap between clinical capacity and the revenue reaching it — leading with sustainability, using appointment volume as the concrete mechanism.

**Priority:** Second-priority vertical, following Higher-Ed. Still small-TAM and quality-over-quantity — measure twice, cut once.

**Sequence:** Cold call → Voicemail → LinkedIn (manual, no automation) → Email. Always in that order, never skipping a step.

**Standing guardrails:**
- No guarantees, ever.
- Never say "agency," "sales," "customers," or "company." Say organization, mission, community, programs, clients.
- Lead with sustainability and mission continuity; appointment volume is the concrete mechanism underneath it.
- Stay consultative. Never argue an objection; reframe it and hand it back as a question.

## 4. Call Psychology (Why This Script Is Built This Way)

**Objective of the call:** Earn twenty seconds of permission, surface whether funding uncertainty and underused appointment capacity are a live, named problem, and move a genuinely relevant prospect toward a working session — without pitching a service.

**Why it's built this way:** Mental-health nonprofit leaders are exhausted by both the funding environment and by vendors who don't understand the clinical/mission context. The way through is empathy first, sustainability second, and a concrete mechanism (appointment volume) third — never a generic "grow your revenue" pitch. Because this buyer is closer to already thinking in earned-income terms than Higher Ed, sustainability language can appear earlier in the conversation than it would in the Higher-Ed script — but it still has to be earned through a real exchange, not announced in the first line.

## 5. Cold Call Script

### Step 1 — Open: permission, not pitch
> Rep: "Hi [Name] — this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call and you weren't expecting it. Do you have twenty seconds?"

### Step 2 — Bridge to a shared theme
> [If yes] Rep: "Appreciate it. We've been working with organizations like [Org Name] on building more sustainable, unrestricted revenue — a lot of mental-health nonprofits are working harder just to maintain the funding they already have, between grant competition and softer giving. Does that sound familiar for [Org Name]?"

### Step 3 — Ask, don't tell
> Rep: "How much of your growth right now is tied to grants and donor dollars versus revenue you generate directly through client appointments?"

### Step 4 — Summarize, then ask again
> Rep: [Reflect back in one sentence — e.g.,] "So it sounds like clinical demand is there, but the revenue side hasn't kept pace with it." "Is that becoming a bigger conversation internally — for the board, for staffing, or both?"

### Step 5 — Let it expand, summarize once more
> [Prospect expands] Rep: "That's consistent with what we're hearing — a lot of teams want to build more of an earned-income engine so a single grant decision or a slow giving season doesn't put the mission at risk."

### Step 6 — Optional proof (one sentence, only if it fits)
See Section 7. Insert at most one, right here, only if natural — and only the generic (unnamed) version unless the named case study has been cleared for external use.

### Step 7 — Invite to a working session, not a pitch
> Rep: "Would it be worth putting fifteen minutes on the calendar — not a pitch, just a working session to look at whether closing that gap is realistic for [Org Name]?"

**Why it works:** The prospect names their own funding pressure and appointment gap before the rep proposes anything. By Step 7, the meeting ask lands as a natural next step in a conversation the prospect has been driving.

**Alternative opener (referral / warm angle):**
> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact — we've been working with a mental-health organization similar in size and mission to [Org Name], and I thought of you given the overlap. Got twenty seconds?"

## 6. Gatekeeper Version

**Objective:** Get past the front desk or intake coordinator without sounding like a vendor cold call, and without asking them to judge something they're not equipped to judge.

> Rep: "Hi, this is [Rep] — quick one for you. Who handles growth or development strategy over there, [Name] or someone else?"

> [If routed to voicemail or told to email] Rep: "That's great, thank you — could I get [Name]'s direct line, or should I go through you for a callback?"

> [If pressed on reason for calling] Rep: "Sure — we help mental-health nonprofits build sustainable revenue outside of grants and donations, so more of the mission is funded on their own terms. I just want thirty seconds with whoever owns that."

**Why it works:** A direct routing question gives the gatekeeper nothing to screen, and naming a real function ("growth or development strategy") signals legitimacy without naming a sales motive.

**Note per Damon:** he has confirmed he is willing to take a meeting with someone who reports directly to the actual decision-maker, not only the decision-maker themselves.

## 7. Voicemail

**Objective:** Leave a message that earns an open on the follow-up email, not one that tries to close on its own.

> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. We've been working with organizations like [Org Name] on building sustainable, unrestricted revenue — enrollments, appointments, membership, whatever that looks like for you — without adding headcount. I'll send a short email over so you have something concrete to look at — if it's relevant, I'd welcome fifteen minutes. Talk soon."

**Why it works:** No cliffhanger, no false urgency — consistent with the client's professional, no-guarantees tone.

## 8. Objection Handling

Never argue, never "overcome" — reframe in a sentence or two and hand it back as a question.

| Objection | Response | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not budgeted for this fiscal year." | "That's what most organizations tell us at first — until they see it tends to pay for itself once it's running, so it becomes less of a new cost and more of a new, unrestricted revenue line. Is budget the only piece, or is there more to it?" | Reframes cost as self-funding rather than a new expense; hands the conversation back with a question. |
| "We already have a marketing team / an agency." | "Good — sounds like that's getting some attention already. Most of what we see internally or from agencies is brand and donor communications; we sit in a narrower lane — specifically appointment volume and client-access revenue. Is that piece actually covered today, or more of a gap?" | Doesn't position Earned Impact as a replacement, avoiding loyalty-based defensiveness; narrows to the specific gap. |
| "Our team is too busy / doesn't have the bandwidth." | "That tracks — most teams we talk to are already stretched thin, especially clinically. The way clients tend to think about it is less 'another project' and more a way to fill capacity you're already paying for, without adding headcount. Does bandwidth feel like the real blocker, or is it more that you haven't tried something like this before?" | Reuses the "without adding headcount" framing; separates the stated objection from what might be underneath it. |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to send something over. So it's actually useful and not generic — what's the bigger gap right now: appointment volume, or the funding mix behind it?" | Complies first (builds trust), then extracts one more qualifying detail. |
| "We're not looking right now." | "Totally fair, no pressure. Would it make sense to check back in a few months, or is there a point in your grant or budget cycle that's better timing?" | Protects the relationship for future re-engagement instead of forcing a close. |

## 9. Discovery Questions

Choose two to four total, based on where the prospect showed energy. Never work through the full list on one call.

**Current State**
- What does a strong month look like for you right now in terms of client appointments?
- What's changed on that front over the last year?

**Revenue Model**
- How much of your current growth is grants and donor-driven versus revenue you generate directly through client appointments?
- How has that mix shifted over the last few years?

**Capacity**
- Is there open clinical capacity right now that isn't being filled, or is the waitlist the bigger issue?
- If appointment volume grew, could your clinical team actually absorb it?

**Growth Challenges**
- What's been the biggest challenge in growing the appointment/earned-revenue side?
- What's gotten in the way in the past when you've tried?

**Decision Process**
- Who else would need to be involved if this moved forward — the board, another director?
- How do decisions like this typically get made on your end?

**Timing**
- Is this a live priority, or more of a next-budget-cycle conversation?
- Has funding uncertainty made this a bigger internal conversation recently?

## 10. Qualification Checklist

An appointment only counts as qualified if every box below is checked.

- [ ] US-based nonprofit (Canada only if explicitly pre-approved)
- [ ] Annual budget between $1M and $50M
- [ ] Org type: mental-health therapy nonprofit
- [ ] Has an active marketing budget or a clear willingness to allocate one
- [ ] Has open clinical capacity to absorb new client appointments
- [ ] Contact is Executive Director, Clinical Director, Director of Development/Operations, **or** someone who reports directly to one of those roles
- [ ] Pain point (appointment shortfall, funding uncertainty, or capacity gap) confirmed in the prospect's own words
- [ ] No conflicting exclusivity with a competing agency for this specific offer

**Soft qualification guidance** (internal read-checks, not verbatim questions):
- **Authority** — talking with someone who can move this forward, or would this loop in the board first?
- **Budget** — room to invest now, or would this need to be built into next year's plan?
- **Need** — did the pain point come from them, in their own words?
- **Timing** — live priority, or something on a future horizon?
- **Capacity** — could clinical staff actually absorb more appointments if this worked?

## 11. Meeting Transition

**Objective:** Make the invitation feel like a continuation of the sustainability conversation the prospect has been driving.

> Rep: "Based on what you're describing, this sounds worth a real conversation rather than me guessing on a cold call. I'd like to put you together with [Name/Title] for a working session — they'll walk through what closing a gap like this has looked like for a mental-health organization your size, and you can decide from there whether it's worth pursuing. I've got [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] — which works better?"

**Why it works:** "Working session" and "you can decide" keep the frame consultative, and the comparison stays to "a mental-health organization your size" unless a named case study has been cleared for use.
