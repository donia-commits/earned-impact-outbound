## 1. ICP Psychology

Before this buyer picks up the phone, here's what's already running in their head.

**What they wake up thinking about:** Whether this term's or next term's numbers will hit the number the board or the president set. Enrollment is not an abstract metric to this buyer — it is the budget. A shortfall in deposits directly threatens payroll, program viability, and their own job security. They are also, most days, doing the job of two or three people: admissions marketing, web updates, social media, sometimes event planning and alumni communications, all at once.

**What success looks like to them:** A full cohort. A program that hits its target class size instead of running under-enrolled. A pipeline of prospective students that doesn't dry up every time they lose a staff member or a budget line. Success is measured in application counts, yield rate, and deposits — not in revenue diversification language.

**Why they'd take a cold call at all:** Because "we help you build enrollment capacity you don't currently have the internal bandwidth to build" solves a problem they are personally accountable for, this week — not a strategic problem they'll get to eventually. Compare that to "grow unrestricted revenue," which sounds like a finance conversation they'd forward to someone else.

**The language they naturally use:** Enrollment, admissions, prospective students, pipeline, applications, yield, deposits, cohort, program fill, marketing capacity, "wearing too many hats," "we don't have anyone dedicated to this." They do **not** naturally say "unrestricted revenue," "earned income," or "donor dependency" — those are true underneath, but not how this buyer frames their own problem. Per Kaelene's guidance: Higher Ed prospects come to us because they're resource-constrained and student flow is dropping; increasing enrollment requires marketing resources they don't have, and we function as **a marketing team for the price of one hire.** Unrestricted revenue is the mechanism; enrollment is the outcome they're chasing.

**What would make them defensive:** Being told their enrollment strategy is failing, being compared to "agencies," or being pitched services before they've said what's actually going wrong. This buyer has likely already fielded three vendor calls this month promising more applications through some ad platform.

## 2. Campaign Overview

**Objective:** Book a working session with a Higher-Ed decision-maker (or someone who reports directly to one) whose enrollment pipeline shows a real, current gap — using enrollment and marketing-capacity language throughout, never unrestricted-revenue language.

**Priority:** This is Earned Impact's primary initial vertical. The addressable list is small (~2,500 accounts per current targeting) — every dial matters more than usual. Measure twice, cut once: quality of the conversation over volume of dials.

**Sequence:** Cold call → Voicemail → LinkedIn (manual, no automation) → Email. Always in that order, never skipping a step.

**Standing guardrails:**
- No guarantees, ever.
- Never say "agency," "sales," "customers," or "company." Say organization, mission, programs, prospective students.
- Never lead with unrestricted revenue — lead with enrollment and marketing capacity.
- Stay consultative. Never argue an objection; reframe it and hand it back as a question.

## 4. Call Psychology (Why This Script Is Built This Way)

**Objective of the call:** Earn twenty seconds of permission, surface whether enrollment or marketing capacity is a live, named problem, and move a genuinely relevant prospect toward a working session — without ever pitching a service.

**Why it's built this way:** Higher-Ed leaders are pattern-trained to hang up on vendor pitches about "boosting enrollment through digital marketing" — they've heard it from every ad agency and consultant chasing the same small pool of institutions. The way through is to sound like someone who already understands the operational reality of a small marketing team, not like someone selling a platform. The opener stays entirely in enrollment and capacity language; unrestricted revenue is never mentioned on the call — it's the mechanism underneath, not the message.

---

## 5. Cold Call Script

### Step 1 — Open: permission, not pitch
> Rep: "Hi [Name] — this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call and you weren't expecting it. Do you have twenty seconds?"

### Step 2 — Bridge to enrollment reality (never revenue)
> [If yes] Rep: "Appreciate it. We've been working with organizations like [Org Name] on the enrollment side — a lot of small colleges and universities are running lean marketing teams that are stretched across admissions, communications, and everything in between. Does that sound familiar for [Org Name]?"

### Step 3 — Ask, don't tell
> Rep: "How's enrollment tracking against where you'd want it to be right now — and is your team resourced to go after the gap, or is it more that you don't have the bandwidth to chase it properly?"

### Step 4 — Summarize, then ask again
> Rep: [Reflect back in one sentence — e.g.,] "So it sounds like [Program/overall enrollment] is behind target, and your team's already stretched thin trying to close that on top of everything else." "Is that becoming a bigger conversation with the president or the board?"

### Step 5 — Let it expand, summarize once more
> [Prospect expands] Rep: "That tracks with what we're hearing from other small institutions — teams want to grow enrollment without having to build out an entire marketing department to do it."

### Step 6 — Optional proof (one sentence, only if it fits)
See Section 7. Insert at most one, right here, only if natural — and only the generic (unnamed) version unless a specific case study has been cleared for external use.

### Step 7 — Invite to a working session, not a pitch
> Rep: "Would it be worth putting fifteen minutes on the calendar — not a pitch, just a working session to look at where the enrollment gap actually is and whether there's something worth pursuing?"

**Why it works:** Every step stays inside enrollment and capacity language, which is the buyer's own frame. By the time the meeting is asked for, the prospect has been describing their own problem in their own words — the rep hasn't introduced "unrestricted revenue" once.

**Alternative opener (referral / warm angle):**
> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact — we've been working with a small university similar in size to [Org Name], and I thought of you given the overlap. Got twenty seconds?"

## 6. Gatekeeper / Wrong-Person Version

**Important — this is not a receptionist script.** Per Kaelene's guidance: at most Higher-Ed institutions we will not hit a formal gatekeeper. The much more common scenario is reaching *someone* in a one-to-three-person marketing or communications office — and because that team is so small, the "wrong person" you reach is very often still relevant, sometimes the actual buyer, sometimes someone who reports directly to them. Treat this as a triage conversation, not a bypass.

**If you reach a marketing/communications generalist who may not be the final decision-maker:**
> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. Quick question — are you the one who owns enrollment marketing strategy day to day, or does that sit with someone else on your team?"

> [If they own a piece of it] Rep: "Got it — that's exactly who I was hoping to reach. We've been working with small colleges and universities on enrollment capacity — is that something you're able to talk through, or should this include [President/Provost/VP] as well?"

> [If they say it's someone else, e.g. the President or a VP] Rep: "That's helpful, thank you. Would you be open to a quick two-minute rundown so I'm not cold-calling them blind, or would you rather I reach out directly?"

**If actually screened by an assistant/front desk (smaller schools, president's office):**
> Rep: "Hi, this is [Rep] — quick one for you. Who handles enrollment or marketing strategy over there, [Name] or someone else?"
> [If pressed on reason for calling] Rep: "Sure — we work with small colleges and universities on building enrollment capacity their internal team doesn't have the bandwidth to cover. I just want a few minutes with whoever owns that."

**Why it works:** It never assumes the person on the line is irrelevant — a real risk at institutions this size — and it gives them a legitimate, low-pressure way to either engage or route you, without making them screen a sales pitch they're not equipped to judge.

**Note per Damon:** he has confirmed he is willing to take a meeting with someone who reports directly to the actual decision-maker, not only the decision-maker themselves. Qualification does not require reaching the president/VP on the first call.

## 7. Voicemail

**Objective:** Leave a message that earns an open on the follow-up email, in enrollment language, with no ask beyond "expect an email."

> Rep: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with Earned Impact. We've been working with small colleges and universities on enrollment capacity — closing the gap between where enrollment is and where it needs to be, without adding headcount to your marketing team. I'll send a short email over so you have something concrete to look at — if it's relevant, I'd welcome fifteen minutes. Talk soon."

**Why it works:** No cliffhanger, no urgency, and it names the buyer's actual problem (enrollment capacity) rather than a service category.

## 8. Objection Handling

Never argue, never "overcome" — reframe in a sentence or two and hand it back as a question.

| Objection | Response | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| "We're not budgeted for this fiscal year." | "That's what most schools tell us at first — until they see it tends to pay for itself once enrollment picks up, so it becomes less of a new line item and more of a way to hit the number you're already being measured on. Is budget the only piece, or is there more to it?" | Reframes cost as self-funding through enrollment growth, not a new expense; hands the conversation back with a question. |
| "We already have a marketing team / an agency." | "Good — sounds like that's getting some attention already. Most of what we see internally or from agencies is broader brand and communications work; we sit in a narrower lane — specifically enrollment pipeline and admissions marketing capacity. Is that piece fully covered today, or more of a gap even with what you have?" | Doesn't position Earned Impact as a replacement, avoiding loyalty-based defensiveness; narrows to the specific gap. |
| "Our team is stretched too thin to take on anything new." | "That's actually the exact reason schools bring us in — the idea isn't to hand your team another project, it's to be a marketing team for the price of less than one hire, so you're not adding headcount to close the gap. Does bandwidth feel like the real blocker, or is it more that you haven't tried something like this before?" | Uses Kaelene's "marketing team for the price of one" framing directly; separates the stated objection from what's underneath it. |
| "Send me some information." | "Happy to send something over. So it's actually useful and not generic — what's the bigger gap right now: overall enrollment, a specific program, or your team's capacity to go after either?" | Complies first (builds trust), then extracts one more qualifying detail instead of letting the call end with nothing. |
| "We're not looking right now." | "Totally fair, no pressure. Would it make sense to check back closer to your next enrollment cycle, or is there a better point in your planning calendar?" | Protects the relationship for re-engagement instead of forcing a close. |

## 9. Discovery Questions

Choose two to four total, based on where the prospect showed energy. Never work through the full list on one call.

**Current State**
- What does a strong enrollment cycle look like for you right now — applications, deposits, or a specific program filling?
- What's changed on that front over the last year or two?

**Marketing Capacity**
- How is enrollment marketing handled today — is there a dedicated person or team, or does it get split across other roles?
- If enrollment grew faster than expected, could your team actually keep up with the marketing and outreach load?

**Growth Challenges**
- What's been the biggest obstacle in closing the enrollment gap so far?
- What's gotten in the way in the past when you've tried to fix it?

**Decision Process**
- Besides you, who else is usually part of a decision like this — the president, the board, someone else?
- How do decisions like this typically move forward on your end?

**Timing**
- Is this a live priority heading into your next enrollment cycle, or more of a future-budget-cycle conversation?
- Has this become a bigger conversation internally recently?

## 10. Qualification Checklist

An appointment only counts as qualified if every box below is checked.

- [ ] US-based institution (Canada only if explicitly pre-approved)
- [ ] Annual budget between $1M and $50M
- [ ] Org type: small/micro-university or college, <2,000 students
- [ ] Has an active marketing budget or a clear willingness to allocate one
- [ ] Has enrollment/admissions capacity to absorb new applicants if the pipeline grew
- [ ] Contact is President, Provost, VP of Enrollment/Marketing, Director of Admissions, **or** someone who reports directly to one of those roles
- [ ] Pain point (enrollment gap, program under-filling, or marketing-capacity shortfall) confirmed in the prospect's own words
- [ ] No conflicting exclusivity with a competing agency for this specific offer

**Soft qualification guidance** (internal read-checks, not verbatim questions):
- **Authority** — talking with someone who can move this forward, or would this loop in the president/board first?
- **Budget** — room to invest now, or would this need to be built into next year's plan?
- **Need** — did the enrollment pain point come from them, in their own words?
- **Timing** — live priority tied to an upcoming enrollment cycle, or a future horizon?
- **Capacity** — could admissions actually absorb more applicants/enrolled students if this worked?

## 11. Meeting Transition

**Objective:** Make the invitation feel like a continuation of the enrollment conversation the prospect has been driving.

> Rep: "Based on what you're describing, this sounds worth a real conversation rather than me guessing on a cold call. I'd like to put you together with [Name/Title] for a working session — they'll walk through what closing a gap like this has looked like for a school your size, and you can decide from there whether it's worth pursuing. I've got [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] — which works better?"

**Why it works:** "Working session" and "you can decide" keep the frame consultative, and the comparison stays to "a school your size," not a named account unless cleared for use.

## 16. QA Notes

- Score every recorded call against the rhythm in Section 5: open → bridge → ask → summarize → expand → (optional proof) → invite.
- Flag any call where "unrestricted revenue," "unrestricted funds," or "earned income" is used in the first two minutes — this is a Higher-Ed-specific violation of the required frame.

- Confirm the gatekeeper/wrong-person triage in Section 6 was used correctly — reps should not assume a small-team contact is irrelevant.
